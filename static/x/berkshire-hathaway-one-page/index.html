从纺织厂到多元控股集团

  1. 1955–1965

    纺织公司合并，巴菲特取得控制权

    两家新英格兰纺织企业在 1955 年合并，沿用至今的公司名称由此而来。1965 年，巴菲特合伙企业取得控制权。
    五十周年回顾

  2. 1967

    收购 National Indemnity

    伯克希尔用纺织业务产生的资金进入财产与意外险。保险浮存金此后成为集团能够长期运用的一项资金来源。
    1985 年股东信

  3. 1972–1985

    收购 See’s，退出纺织

    1972 年，巴菲特和查理·芒格控制的 Blue Chip Stamps 收购 See’s Candies。See’s 所需营运资本较少，集团可以把盈余投向其他业务。1985 年，伯克希尔关闭纺织业务。
    See’s 收购回顾 · 纺织业务回顾

  4. 1996–1998

    扩张直保与再保险

    1996 年完成对 GEICO 的全资收购，扩大车险直销业务。同年发行 B 股。1998 年并入 General Re，扩大再保险业务。
    1998 年股东信 · 股东手册

  5. 2000–2010

    能源与铁路成为支柱

    2000 年投资 MidAmerican Energy，为今天的伯克希尔能源业务奠定基础。2010 年收购尚未持有的 BNSF 股份，把大型货运铁路纳入集团。
    MidAmerican 交易 · BNSF 收购

  6. 2026

    管理层交接，继续收购实业

    Greg Abel 接任 CEO，Howard G. Buffett 随后接任董事长。集团同年收购 OxyChem 和 Taylor Morrison，扩大化工与住宅开发业务。

GEICO 承保利润承压

前三季已赚保费同比增长 2.4%，税前承保利润降至 33.92 亿美元，同比下降 41.2%。按赔付及理赔费用加承保费用除以已赚保费计算，综合成本率由 82.6% 升至 90.0%。更高的出险频率和案均赔付、较低的单均保费，以及佣金和广告费用上升挤压了利润。

BNSF 运量和运营效率改善

前三季运量同比增长 4.2%，每车或每单位收入增长 7.1%，部分来自燃油附加费。按铁路运营费用除以运营收入计算，运营比率由 65.6% 降至 64.9%。同期 BNSF 和伯克希尔能源合计投入约 102 亿美元资本开支，占集团约 63%。

能源利润增长，山火责任仍有不确定性

伯克希尔能源前三季归属母公司股东利润为 36.17 亿美元，同比增长 10.0%，主要增长来自美国公用事业和天然气管道。PacifiCorp 截至 9 月底就相关山火累计计提约 28.5 亿美元损失，最终损失仍可能高于已计提金额。

流动性、负债与每股表现

2026 年 9 月底，归属伯克希尔股东权益为 7,613.68 亿美元，票据及其他借款合计 1,307.83 亿美元，其中铁路、公用事业和能源板块为 850.16 亿美元。公司披露不为 BNSF 与伯克希尔能源及其子公司的借款提供担保。前三季每股经营利润上升，但经营现金流减资本开支同比下降 11.3%，两项指标应结合观察。

集团如何决定回购

CEO 与董事长协商后，若保守估计的内在价值高于股价，可回购 A 股或 B 股。公司规定回购后合并现金、现金等价物及美国国库券持有额须至少为 300 亿美元。三季报 · 股份回购政策

哪些变量会影响回报

重大灾害会推高保险赔付，利率下行会压低短债再投资收益，股票持仓集中会放大净利润波动。铁路和公用事业持续需要投入资本，收购和回购的价格也会影响长期回报。

From textile mills to a diversified holding company

  1. 1955–1965

    A textile merger and a change of control

    Two New England textile businesses merged in 1955 to form Berkshire Hathaway. Buffett Partnership took control in 1965.
    50th-anniversary retrospective

  2. 1967

    National Indemnity acquisition

    Berkshire used cash from textiles to enter property and casualty insurance. Insurance float became a lasting source of funds the group could invest.
    1985 shareholder letter

  3. 1972–1985

    See’s acquisition and the exit from textiles

    Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger controlled Blue Chip Stamps when it bought See’s Candies in 1972. See’s needed little working capital, leaving cash available for investment elsewhere. Berkshire shut down its textile operations in 1985.
    See’s acquisition retrospective · Textile operations retrospective

  4. 1996–1998

    Expansion in direct insurance and reinsurance

    Berkshire took full ownership of GEICO in 1996, expanding its direct auto insurance business. It issued Class B shares that year and added General Re in 1998.
    1998 shareholder letter · Owner’s Manual

  5. 2000–2010

    Energy and rail become core businesses

    The 2000 investment in MidAmerican Energy laid the foundation for Berkshire Hathaway Energy. Berkshire bought the remaining BNSF shares in 2010, bringing a major freight railroad into the group.
    MidAmerican transaction · BNSF acquisition

  6. 2026

    Leadership transition and new acquisitions

    Greg Abel became CEO, and Howard G. Buffett later became chairman. Berkshire acquired OxyChem and Taylor Morrison, expanding its chemicals and homebuilding businesses.
    Chairman transition announcement · Acquisition disclosures

GEICO underwriting margins narrowed

Nine-month earned premiums rose 2.4% year over year, while pre-tax underwriting earnings fell 41.2% to $3.392B. The combined ratio, losses and loss adjustment expenses plus underwriting expenses divided by earned premiums, rose from 82.6% to 90.0%. More frequent and severe claims, lower premiums per policy, and higher commission and advertising costs squeezed earnings.

BNSF improved volumes and operating efficiency

Nine-month freight volumes rose 4.2% year over year and revenue per car or unit rose 7.1%, partly from fuel surcharges. BNSF’s operating ratio, railroad operating expenses divided by operating revenue, fell from 65.6% to 64.9%. BNSF and Berkshire Hathaway Energy together spent about $10.2B on capex, roughly 63% of the group total.

Energy earnings rose and wildfire exposure remains

Berkshire Hathaway Energy’s nine-month earnings attributable to Berkshire shareholders rose 10.0% to $3.617B, led by U.S. utilities and natural gas pipelines. At the end of September, PacifiCorp had accrued about $2.85B in cumulative estimated losses for the wildfires. Ultimate losses could exceed that amount.

Liquidity, debt and per-share results

At Sep 30, 2026, equity attributable to Berkshire shareholders was $761.368B. Notes payable and other borrowings totaled $130.783B, including $85.016B in Railroad, Utilities and Energy. Berkshire reports that it does not guarantee borrowings of BNSF, Berkshire Hathaway Energy or their subsidiaries. Nine-month operating earnings per share rose while operating cash flow less capex fell 11.3% year over year.

  • Buying businesses. Berkshire acquired OxyChem for about $9.4B and paid roughly $6.8B in cash for Taylor Morrison in 2026.
  • Holding equity securities. Cash spent on equity purchases less proceeds from sales was $11.199B in the first nine months of 2026, versus net sales of $10.599B a year earlier. As of Sep 30, 2026, the five largest holdings below represented 65% of the reported fair value of equity securities.

  • Buying back shares. Berkshire reported about $9.6B of repurchases in Q3 and $14.4B in the first nine months of 2026. It also holds short-term Treasury bills to help meet claims and fund acquisitions and other needs.

How Berkshire decides to repurchase shares

The CEO may repurchase Class A or Class B shares below conservatively estimated intrinsic value, after consulting the chairman. Berkshire requires at least $30B of consolidated cash, cash equivalents and U.S. Treasury bills after repurchases. Q3 Form 10-Q · Repurchase policy

What can affect returns

Catastrophes raise insurance claims, falling rates reduce yields on reinvested Treasury bills, and concentrated equity holdings add volatility to net earnings. Rail and utilities need ongoing capital investment, and acquisition and repurchase prices affect long-term returns.
