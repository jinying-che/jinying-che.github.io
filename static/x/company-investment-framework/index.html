A practical guide to investing

好公司，
也要好价格。

投资一家公司，既要理解它如何赚钱、能赚多久，也要判断现在的价格包含了怎样的期待。这份手册帮你把复杂问题拆成可验证的步骤。

The framework

从五个问题开始

先判断企业值不值得长期关注，再判断买入价格是否留出了足够的余地。每个问题都需要证据，而不仅是一个好故事。

判断顺序：看得懂生意 → 检查财务与管理 → 估计价值区间 → 评估风险与价格。遇到无法解释的重大疑点，可以先停下来继续研究。
Business quality

先弄清楚，公司如何赚钱

不急着看股价。先用自己的话解释：客户为什么付钱，这门生意未来是否仍有需求，以及竞争对手为什么难以抢走它。

PROFIT MODEL

收入从哪里来？

产品与客户是谁？收入来自一次性销售、持续订阅还是交易抽成？哪些业务贡献了收入与利润，客户是否过度集中？

INDUSTRY

行业空间有多大？

需求是长期增长、周期波动还是逐渐萎缩？增长来自用户增加、提价、市场份额提升，还是一次性并购？

COMPETITION

优势能持续多久？

留意品牌、成本、网络效应、转换成本、技术与渠道。用毛利率、留存、份额和提价能力等证据检验优势。

UNIT ECONOMICS

增长是否创造价值？

多卖一份产品能留下多少利润？获得新客户的成本是否合理？如果增长需要不断补贴或投入大量资本，回报可能有限。

一个简单检验：

假设股市明天关闭三年，你是否仍愿意持有这门生意？如果连主要客户、盈利来源和竞争威胁都说不清，先把它放进“继续研究”。

Financial quality

利润之外，更要看现金

把财报当成一组相互印证的证据。尽量看过去 3—5 年及最新财报，结合行业周期和同行比较，而不是盯住单一指标。

收入与增长Revenue growth

收入增长率 = 本期收入 ÷ 上期收入 − 1拆分销量、价格、并购与汇率影响；增长是否可持续，客户或单一产品是否过于集中？

盈利能力Margins

毛利率 = 毛利润 ÷ 收入；营业利润率 = 营业利润 ÷ 收入观察趋势和同行差异，弄清利润变化来自定价、成本、费用控制还是一次性项目。

资本回报ROIC

投入资本回报率 ≈ 税后营业利润 ÷ 平均投入资本衡量投入经营的资本创造了多少回报，并与资本成本及同行比较；计算口径需保持一致。

现金创造Cash flow

简化自由现金流 ≈ 经营现金流 − 资本开支结合净利润、应收账款和存货看现金转化。现金流可能受营运资金与投资周期影响，不宜仅看一年。

资产负债表Balance sheet

净债务 ≈ 有息债务 − 可动用现金关注债务到期时间、利息覆盖、再融资能力和受限现金；净债务 / EBITDA 可辅助比较，但不适用于所有公司。

每股价值Per share

每股收益 = 归属普通股股东的净利润 ÷ 加权平均普通股股数关注股权激励、增发、可转债和回购对稀释后股数的影响。公司增长，不一定等于每股价值同步增长。

口径提醒：这里的“经营现金流减资本开支”是简化观察口径；做企业自由现金流折现时，应使用与资本成本匹配、剔除融资影响的现金流。银行、保险等金融企业应另看资本充足率、资产质量等行业指标。

People & governance

好生意，也需要可靠的掌舵人

管理层的价值不只体现在讲述愿景，更体现在过去如何对待股东、使用现金以及解释错误。

重点观察什么

  • 诚信与透明：承诺能否兑现？遇到问题是否及时、清楚地披露？
  • 资本配置：投资、并购、回购和分红是否有清晰逻辑及可检验的结果？
  • 激励与利益：薪酬考核是否关注长期回报？管理层持股和股东利益是否一致？
  • 治理结构：董事会是否有效监督？控股股东、关联交易与少数股东权益如何？

值得追问的信号

  • 业绩目标反复更改，解释前后不一致。
  • 频繁高价并购，却难以证明并购回报。
  • 关联交易、资金占用或股权质押存在疑点。
  • 股权激励规模很大，每股价值却长期没有改善。
  • 审计意见异常、关键人员频繁离职或披露不透明。

从哪里找证据？年报中的公司治理与薪酬章节、审计意见、历史公告、业绩会记录，以及过去承诺与实际结果的对照。

Valuation & expectations

好公司，不代表任何价格都值得买

估值是将未来假设转换成今天的价格区间。与其寻找唯一的“正确价格”，不如明确市场期待、关键假设和它们出错后的影响。

相对估值

市盈率 / PE

适合利润相对稳定、同行可比较的公司。

市盈率 = 当前股价 ÷ 每股收益
估算股价 = 预期每股收益 × 假设市盈率

比较同行与自身历史时，要统一利润口径、时间和币种。周期高点利润、一次性收益或亏损可能让 PE 失真；低 PE 本身不等于便宜。

绝对估值

现金流折现 / DCF

适合能对长期现金流作出合理区间估计的公司。

企业价值 = 预测企业自由现金流与终值的现值
股权价值 ≈ 企业价值 − 净债务 − 少数股东权益等

再除以稀释后股数得到每股价值。企业自由现金流通常使用加权平均资本成本折现。增长、折现率和终值都很敏感；若直接折现股权自由现金流，得到的是股权价值，不应再扣一次净债务。

方法要与公司匹配：周期性企业可研究跨周期的正常化利润；亏损企业可结合市销率、单位经济与盈利路径；银行、保险等需结合市净率、资本充足率和资产质量。任何单一倍数都不能独立决定买卖。

用“安全边际”应对不确定性

Risk & decision

投资前，先想清楚什么会错

把风险写成具体情景，估计发生后对盈利、现金流和估值的影响。信息缺口本身也会增加不确定性。

经营风险

需求下滑、竞争加剧、产品替代、客户流失或成本上升，可能让增长和利润低于预期。

财务与治理风险

高债务、现金周转恶化、融资受阻、财务披露异常或侵害中小股东权益。

外部与估值风险

监管、利率、汇率、技术变化或估值倍数下降，即使盈利增长也可能影响回报。

把结论落实到行动

写下买入逻辑、关键假设、合理价值区间、最重要的反证及持续观察指标。仓位应结合自身承受亏损的能力、持有期限与组合分散情况；当关键假设失效或价格明显偏离价值时，重新评估。

Start with five questions

Business quality

First, understand how it makes money

Before looking at the share price, explain in your own words why customers pay, whether demand will persist, and why competitors cannot easily take the business away.

PROFIT MODEL

Where does revenue come from?

Who are the customers and what are the products? Are sales one-off, subscription-based or transaction fees? Which businesses generate revenue and profit, and is customer concentration excessive?

INDUSTRY

How much opportunity is there?

Is demand growing over the long term, cyclical or shrinking? Does growth come from more customers, higher prices, market-share gains or one-off acquisitions?

COMPETITION

How durable is the advantage?

Consider brand, cost, network effects, switching costs, technology and distribution. Test the case against margins, retention, market share and pricing power.

UNIT ECONOMICS

Does growth create value?

How much profit does an additional sale leave? Is customer acquisition economical? Growth that requires continual subsidies or large capital outlays may deliver limited returns.

A simple test:

Imagine the stock market closes for three years. Would you still be comfortable owning this business? If you cannot explain its customers, earnings and competitive threats, keep researching.

Financial quality

Look beyond profits to cash

Treat financial statements as evidence that should corroborate other evidence. Review the past three to five years and the latest results; compare peers and consider the industry cycle rather than relying on one metric.

Revenue and growthRevenue growth

Revenue growth = current revenue ÷ prior revenue − 1Separate the effects of volume, price, acquisitions and exchange rates. Is growth sustainable? Is the company overly dependent on a customer or product?

ProfitabilityMargins

Gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue; operating margin = operating profit ÷ revenueStudy trends and peer differences. Distinguish pricing, costs and expense control from one-off items.

Return on capitalROIC

Return on invested capital ≈ after-tax operating profit ÷ average invested capitalAssess the return generated on operating capital against its cost and peers, using consistent definitions.

Cash generationCash flow

Simplified free cash flow ≈ operating cash flow − capital expenditureCompare cash flow with net income, receivables and inventory. Working-capital and investment cycles can distort a single year.

Balance sheetDebt

Net debt ≈ interest-bearing debt − available cashCheck maturities, interest coverage, refinancing capacity and restricted cash. Net debt / EBITDA can help comparisons but does not suit every company.

Per-share valuePer share

EPS = net income attributable to common shareholders ÷ weighted-average common sharesConsider dilution from stock compensation, new shares and convertible debt, as well as buybacks. Company growth does not automatically translate into equal per-share growth.

Definitions matter: operating cash flow less capital expenditure is a simplified measure. A firm DCF requires cash flow that excludes financing effects and matches the chosen discount rate. Banks and insurers require additional industry measures, such as capital adequacy and asset quality.

People & governance

A good business needs reliable leadership

Management's value is demonstrated not only by its vision, but by how it has treated shareholders, deployed cash and explained mistakes.

What to examine

  • Integrity and transparency: Were promises kept? Are problems disclosed promptly and clearly?
  • Capital allocation: Do investment, acquisitions, buybacks and dividends have a clear rationale and measurable results?
  • Incentives and alignment: Does compensation reward long-term returns? Do management ownership and shareholder interests align?
  • Governance: Is board oversight effective? Examine controlling shareholders, related-party transactions and minority rights.

Signals worth investigating

  • Targets repeatedly change or explanations contradict earlier statements.
  • Frequent expensive acquisitions without evidence of returns.
  • Questionable related-party transactions, use of company funds or pledged shares.
  • Large stock awards while per-share value fails to improve over time.
  • Unusual audit opinions, frequent departures of key staff or opaque disclosures.

Evidence can include the governance and compensation sections of annual reports, audit opinions, announcements, earnings calls, and comparisons between past promises and outcomes.

Valuation & expectations

A good company is not attractive at every price

Valuation translates assumptions about the future into a range of present values. Instead of seeking a single “correct” price, state market expectations, your assumptions and the consequences if those assumptions fail.

Relative valuation

Price / earnings (P/E)

Most useful when profits are relatively stable and peers are comparable.

P/E = current share price ÷ earnings per share
Estimated price = forecast EPS × assumed P/E

Use consistent earnings definitions, periods and currencies when comparing peers and history. Peak-cycle earnings, one-off gains or losses can distort P/E; a low ratio alone does not imply a bargain.

Absolute valuation

Discounted cash flow (DCF)

Useful when a reasonable range for long-term cash flows can be estimated.

Enterprise value = present value of forecast firm cash flows and terminal value
Equity value ≈ enterprise value − net debt − minority interests and other claims

Divide by diluted shares for value per share. Firm cash flows are commonly discounted at the weighted average cost of capital. Growth, discount rate and terminal value are highly sensitive; discounting equity cash flows directly gives equity value, so do not deduct net debt again.

Match the method to the company: use normalized earnings across the cycle for cyclical businesses; consider price-to-sales, unit economics and a path to profitability for loss-makers; and include price-to-book, capital adequacy and asset quality for banks and insurers. No single multiple determines an investment decision.

Use a margin of safety for uncertainty

Compare the current price with value ranges based on conservative, base and optimistic assumptions, allowing for estimation error. If estimated value per share is positive, (estimated value − current price) ÷ estimated value measures the discount to that estimate. A larger discount is not automatically safer: check the assumptions and overlooked material risks. No fixed discount suits every company.

Risk & decision

Before investing, ask what could go wrong

Write concrete scenarios and consider their effect on earnings, cash flow and valuation. Gaps in information also add uncertainty.

Business risk

Falling demand, stronger competition, product substitution, lost customers or rising costs can hurt growth and margins.

Financial and governance risk

High debt, deteriorating cash conversion, restricted financing, unusual disclosures or harm to minority shareholders.

External and valuation risk

Regulation, rates, currency, technology or a lower valuation multiple can affect returns even when earnings grow.

Turn conclusions into a decision

Record the thesis, key assumptions, reasonable value range, most important disconfirming evidence and indicators to monitor. Position size should reflect your capacity for loss, time horizon and portfolio diversification. Reassess if a key assumption fails or price moves well away from value.
